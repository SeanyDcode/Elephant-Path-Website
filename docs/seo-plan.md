# SEO plan: helping grieving people find The Elephant Path

Reviewed November 2026. This explains, in plain language, how people find the
site through search today, what was fixed, and what would help most next.

---

## The short version

The technical foundation is strong. Every page has its own title and
description, the site is fast, there's a sitemap, and Google is allowed in.

The main gap is **content**. Google ranks pages for what they *say*, and right
now the site barely talks about grief:

| Page | Words | Mentions of grief, loss, or mourning |
|---|---|---|
| Home | about 200 | 2 |
| Services | about 570 | 8 (all in one card) |
| About | about 1,000 | 7 |
| Library | about 800 | 29 (the most grief-rich page on the site) |
| Blog | 1 post | 0 |

The Home page's browser title says "Online Grief Counseling in Michigan", but
the page itself doesn't use that phrase anywhere a visitor can see it. Google
notices when a title promises something the page doesn't back up.

Searches like "grief counseling Michigan" are dominated by big directories
(Psychology Today, LifeStance, Grow Therapy). A small practice usually can't
outrank them on those broad phrases. It wins in three other ways:

1. **Google Business Profile**, which shows practices on the map and in the
   "near me" results.
2. **Directory profiles that link back to the site** (Psychology Today is
   already there).
3. **Specific grief topics** that the directories don't cover well, such as
   anticipatory grief, grief while caregiving, or grief after a terminal
   diagnosis. Your hospice and oncology background matches these exactly.

## Already fixed (this update)

These changes are invisible to visitors. They give Google clearer facts.

- **Business details for Google** (structured data) now include:
  - a short description of the practice and its grief focus;
  - the town (Clarkston, MI 48348; no street address, as the practice is
    telehealth);
  - the topics you support, taken from the focus areas and "I also support"
    list;
  - payment types;
  - Sandra as a person: LMSW license, University of Michigan and UM–Flint
    degrees, and the Psychology Today and LinkedIn profiles. These are the
    trust signals Google looks for on health websites.
- **Site name:** the home page now tells Google the site is called
  "The Elephant Path", so it can show that name above search results instead
  of the web address.
- **Blog posts** now say who wrote them (linked to the About page), when they
  were last updated, and which website they belong to.
- **The "page not found" page** no longer has a canonical link (a tag that
  marks the main copy of a page), since it's hidden from search anyway.

## Recommended next steps, most helpful first

### 1. Google Business Profile (outside the website; biggest single gain)

When someone searches "grief counselor near me" or "grief therapist
Clarkston", Google shows a map with three practices before any websites. You
can only appear there with a Google Business Profile.

- Set it up as a **service-area business**. This hides the street address.
  List Michigan, or the counties you most want to reach (for example Oakland
  County), as the service area.
- Choose the closest primary category Google offers (look for options like
  "counselor", "psychotherapist", or a grief-specific one if it's listed), and
  add other relevant categories as secondary ones.
- Add the website, phone, hours, and "Online appointments" or "Online care"
  if Google offers it.
- In the description and services list, name the grief work plainly: grief
  and loss, anticipatory grief, caregiver support, end-of-life support.
- Once it's live, send me the link. I'll add it to the footer (the brief
  already calls for this) and to the business details for Google.
- Reviews: many therapists choose not to ask clients for reviews, because of
  confidentiality and professional ethics rules. That's fine. The profile
  still helps without them.

### 2. Search Console and Bing (outside the website; 15 minutes)

I couldn't check the live site from my workspace, so please confirm these:

- In **Google Search Console**, check that the sitemap
  (`https://walktheelephantpath.com/sitemap-index.xml`) is submitted and
  shows "Success", and that the Pages report shows your pages as indexed.
- In Search Console, use **URL Inspection** on the home page and click
  **Request indexing** after this update is merged.
- Set up **Bing Webmaster Tools** using "Import from Google Search Console".
  Bing's results also feed ChatGPT search and Microsoft Copilot, which more
  people now use to ask "who can help me with grief?"
- After a month or two, the Search Console **Performance** report shows the
  exact phrases people searched before they found the site. That tells us
  which topics to write about next.

### 3. A dedicated Grief Counseling page (on the website; needs your approval)

Right now grief has about 70 words inside one card on the Services page. A
page of its own, at an address like `/grief-counseling/`, is the strongest
website change for grief searches. It would cover:

- what grief can look like (in the body, mind, relationships, and sleep);
- the kinds of loss you support (see the list in question 1 below);
- how telehealth grief counseling with you works, anywhere in Michigan;
- a few common questions ("Is it too soon or too late to start?", "Do I need
  a diagnosis?", "What if the person hasn't died yet?");
- links to the grief books in the Library and to grief blog posts.

This adds a new page, so it's your decision. The Services card would stay and
link to it. If you'd like it, I'll draft it in your voice with any new claims
marked [TO CONFIRM] for you to review.

### 4. Small wording changes on existing pages (needs your approval)

- **Home:** add the words "grief counseling" somewhere visible near the top.
  For example, the sentence under the headline could read: "I offer
  compassionate grief counseling and therapy by telehealth for teens and adults
  anywhere in Michigan, so you can get support from a place where you already
  feel at home." Your headline "Simple. Accessible. Individualized care." stays
  as it is.
- **Home:** a link from "grief and loss" in the welcome to the grief section
  (or page), and one to the Library. Links between pages, using descriptive
  words, show Google what each page is about.
- **Services:** link each focus-area card to the matching Library topic (for
  example, "Books on grief and loss"). The Library already supports this.
- **Location:** say once, in visible text, that the practice is based in
  Clarkston and serves all of Michigan by telehealth. People search with town
  and county names, and the site currently doesn't mention Clarkston or Oakland
  County anywhere except a crisis line.
- **Services title:** "Counseling Services & Fees" could become "Grief, Illness
  & Caregiver Counseling and Fees", so it says what you do.

### 5. Blog posts about grief (ongoing; your writing)

The blog is where long-term search traffic grows. One clear, helpful post a
month on a specific question can bring in people for years. Each post works
best when it answers one question that someone might type into Google, uses
those words in the title, and links to the grief page and the Library.

Topic ideas that match your experience (choose any that fit):

- What is anticipatory grief? Grieving someone who is still here
- Caregiver grief: when you're losing someone while caring for them
- Grief after a terminal diagnosis, your own or a loved one's
- Losses that don't involve a death: divorce, health, a move, a job
- Is it normal to still feel this way? There's no timeline for grief
- Getting through the holidays and anniversaries after a loss
- How to support a grieving teen
- What hospice taught me about grief
- When to reach out for grief counseling
- Grief and your body: sleep, appetite, and exhaustion
- Grief at work: going back after a loss

Health writing is held to a higher standard by Google. Your byline, license,
and experience already help. Where a post states a health fact, a link to a
reputable source (as the "Why elephants?" section does) helps more.

### 6. Other places to be listed (outside the website)

Each listing that links back to the site builds trust with Google, and each
is also a place people search directly.

- **Psychology Today:** make sure the profile links to the website, and that
  grief is one of the top three specialties (it is, per the brief).
- Directories that showed up in grief searches: **TherapyDen**, **Theravive**,
  and the **Grief.com** counselor directory.
- **Inclusive Therapists**, for LGBTQ+-affirming care.
- **Local hospices, cancer centers, and funeral homes** in Oakland County often
  keep lists of grief counselors for families. Given your hospice background,
  these may be the most valuable links of all, both for search and for
  referrals.
- Keep the practice name, phone, and website exactly the same everywhere.

## What doesn't need to change

- Page speed, image sizes, mobile layout, and accessibility are all in good
  shape, and good accessibility helps search too.
- The sitemap and robots.txt are set up correctly.
- FAQ "rich results" (the drop-down questions in Google) are now shown only for
  government and major health websites, so there's no need to add special code
  for them. Plain questions and answers on a page still help.
- No "keyword stuffing". Writing naturally, in your voice, about what grieving
  people actually ask works better and reads better.
