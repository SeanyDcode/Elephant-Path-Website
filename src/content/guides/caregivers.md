If you are the main person caring for someone you love, you are doing important and often invisible work. Caregiving can be full of love and meaning. It can also be exhausting, lonely and hard to talk about.

## You matter too

Caregivers often put their own needs last. Over time, that can lead to stress, poor sleep, health problems and burnout. Taking care of yourself isn’t selfish. It helps you keep going, and it helps the person you care for too. The Family Caregiver Alliance shares more on this in [Taking care of YOU: self-care for family caregivers](https://www.caregiver.org/resource/taking-care-you-self-care-family-caregivers/).

## Feelings many caregivers have

It is common to feel guilt, resentment, sadness, worry or grief, sometimes alongside deep love. You may grieve changes in the person you care for, even while they are still here. All of these feelings are normal, and none of them make you a bad caregiver.

## Small steps that can help

- **Ask for specific help.** People often want to help but don’t know how. “Could you sit with Mom on Tuesday afternoon?” is easier to say yes to than “Let me know if you need anything.”
- **Take short breaks.** Even a few minutes outside or a phone call with a friend can help.
- **Look into respite care.** Respite care gives caregivers a planned break while someone else provides care.
- **Keep up with your own health.** Try to keep your own appointments, rest and meals.
- **Find your people.** Caregiver support groups, in person or online, can help you feel less alone.

## Finding help in Michigan

- The [Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov/) can connect you with your local Area Agency on Aging, which can help with services for older adults and their caregivers.
- The [ARCH National Respite Locator](https://archrespite.org/caregiver-resources/respitelocator/) helps you search for respite services near you.

## When to reach out for support

If you feel worn down, overwhelmed or like you’ve lost yourself in caregiving, talking with a counselor can help. Our time together can be a place where you come first.
