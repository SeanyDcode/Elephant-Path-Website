Parenting is one of the most meaningful and demanding jobs there is. Nobody does it perfectly. What matters most is a caring, steady connection, and that includes caring for yourself as a parent.

## Parenting through hard seasons

Illness, grief, divorce, a move or money worries affect the whole family. Children and teens may not have the words for what they feel. Their stress can show up as changes in sleep, mood, behavior or schoolwork. Some things that can help:

- **Keep routines where you can.** Predictable meals, bedtimes and rituals help children feel safe.
- **Tell the truth, simply.** Share honest information in words that fit your child’s age. Children often imagine something worse than the truth.
- **Invite questions and feelings.** Let them know it’s OK to feel sad, angry or confused, and that you’re there to listen.
- **Look after yourself.** Children notice how their parents are doing. Getting support for yourself is a gift to them too.

## Everyday connection

- Spend a little one-on-one time with each child, even 10 minutes.
- Notice and name what they are doing well.
- Stay curious about their world: their friends, interests and worries.
- Repair after hard moments. Saying “I’m sorry I yelled” teaches a powerful lesson.
